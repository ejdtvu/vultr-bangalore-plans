## How do Bangalore, Mumbai and Delhi NCR divide up?

They are three separate regions, not three zones of one. Vultr opened Mumbai first in 2022, then announced Bangalore and Delhi NCR together on September 8, 2022, describing the trio as coverage of "India's three largest cities" with "high-performance, low latency locations across the country." The same announcement claimed Vultr had more regions inside India than AWS or Google; that was the company's positioning in September 2022 and I have no way to confirm it still holds in 2026.

There is no same-city second availability zone to fail over to. If you want redundancy across Indian regions, it has to be Bangalore plus Mumbai, or Bangalore plus Delhi NCR, with your own replication between them.

One indirect signal on local depth: on the same comparison site, Bangalore lists 91 servers from a single provider, Delhi NCR 74 from one, Chennai 39 from one, and Mumbai 1,155 from six. That reflects what one price-comparison site has indexed, not India's actual hosting market, but it does tell you Bangalore's listing is essentially the Vultr catalogue and not much else.

## What do the price steps actually tell you?

The gaps between plan families are consistent, and they point to fairly clear rules.

**At 1 vCPU / 1 GB**, €4.51 buys SATA storage and 1 TB of transfer. Another €0.90 (about 20%) buys NVMe and doubles transfer to 2 TB on the vhp plans, or NVMe with the same 1 TB on vhf. For a static site, a cron box or a small API, the SATA plan is enough and the €0.90 is better spent elsewhere.

**At 1 vCPU / 2 GB**, the cheap option flips: vc2-1c-2gb at €9.02 undercuts both NVMe variants at €10.83 by €1.81. What you give up is NVMe and some transfer (2 TB versus 3 TB on vhp). If your workload is database-ish and disk latency shows up in your traces, pay the €1.81; if it is a queue worker or a cron runner, don't.

**At 2 vCPU / 4 GB**, the gap widens to €3.61 (€18.05 versus €21.66), and vhp throws in 5 TB of transfer against 3 TB. This is the tier where the choice starts to depend on traffic volume rather than disk.

**Optimized Cloud Compute starts at €25.27**, which is a different budget bracket entirely. voc-m-1c-8gb-50s-amd at €36.10 is the shape to look at if you need memory rather than cores: 8 GB on a single vCPU for the same price as 4 vCPU / 8 GB on SATA.

## Is Vultr Bangalore down, or is the status page lying to you?

Check Vultr's own status page first. It carries a dedicated "Bangalore - India" component, so a regional incident shows up as its own line rather than being buried in a global banner.

Third-party aggregators are where the confusion starts. One popular status site currently labels the Bangalore component as "under maintenance," then states there has been "1 user-submitted report of outages in the past 24 hours," and a few lines later says "0 outage reports in the last 24 hours." Both cannot be true. The same page claims monitoring since February 2016 and "more than 1,957 outages that affected Vultr Bangalore users," which is impossible as a Bangalore figure because the region did not exist until September 2022. Those numbers are brand-wide or template text.

The user reports listed on that page are also not about Bangalore. They are login problems, specifically authentication emails that never arrived, filed from California, Texas, New Jersey, Virginia, Illinois, Hamburg, Catalonia and Tunisia. That is an account or email delivery issue, not a compute outage in Karnataka.

So: if your Bangalore instance stops responding, check the official status component, then check whether your own account can log in. Treat a "maintenance" badge on an aggregator as a possible scheduled window until Vultr says otherwise.
